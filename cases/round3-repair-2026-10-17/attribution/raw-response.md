# Competitor and market brief

## Decision and scope
Desk-review draft for a small community library choosing room-reservation software, compared on 2026-10-17. Geography and budget are unknown; CAD pricing does not establish the library's location. All evidence is fictional and supplied in brief.md, S1–S4, dated 2026-10-17. RoomPebble and DeskBell are software alternatives for this decision; the current phone-and-notebook process is the status quo. No product was tested.

## Recommendation
DeskBell offers the clearest documented distinction for this choice: its vendor explicitly attributes automatic SMS booking notices to its system and quotes a library-paid branch subscription (S2). If the library wants automatic SMS notices and can afford the subscription, this supports shortlisting DeskBell, subject to checking actual behavior. It does not demonstrate reduced workload or better reservation outcomes.

RoomPebble's passive notice and confirmation statements establish neither the responsible actor nor delivery channel or automation (S1). Its single user note reports continued phone confirmation (S4); that is relevant counterevidence to an assumption of a fully automatic confirmation workflow, but does not prove the product lacks one. The manual process explicitly uses library phone contact and a notebook (S3), making its channel and record method clearer than RoomPebble's excerpt.

## Choice opportunity and hypothesis
Observed distinction: DeskBell explicitly claims automatic SMS notices, while the current library reports phone contact (S2, S3). Inferred relevance: this could fit a desire to reduce routine calls, if borrowers accept SMS. That desire and any time savings are untested. A defensible comparison statement is “DeskBell explicitly documents automatic SMS booking notices”; “DeskBell eliminates confirmation calls” is unsupported. RoomPebble's unknown details and the user's continued calls prevent treating this as an exclusive capability gap (S1, S4). No library-owned software product or product proof was supplied, so no new positioning claim for such a product is proposed.

## Limitations and next check
The excerpts do not establish reliability, accessibility, borrower preferences, total cost, privacy arrangements, implementation effort or comparative outcomes. The single RoomPebble user is not representative; vendor statements are marketing evidence rather than verified behavior. Omitted capabilities remain unknown. These three alternatives do not describe the whole market.

One low-cost next check: have the library walk both vendors through the same sample reservation and ask them to identify who confirms it, who sends each notice, its channel and automation, and the complete library-paid price for one branch. Record the responses alongside the existing phone workflow. This would test the decisive attribution uncertainty before choosing.
